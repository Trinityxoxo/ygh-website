Soften Your Water, Protect Your Home

Doncaster sits in a hard-water area, and over time that limescale builds up everywhere — in your kettle, your boiler, your taps, and your pipework. A water softener removes the minerals that cause scale, protecting your heating system and leaving your water noticeably kinder on skin, hair, glassware, and appliances.

Yorkshire Green Heating supplies and installs water softeners across Doncaster, and we handle the kitchen plumbing that goes with them. Because we're also gas and heating engineers, we understand exactly how hard water damages boilers and radiators — and how the right softener keeps your whole system running efficiently for longer.

The Benefits of a Water Softener

Less Limescale, Lower Bills

Scale is the enemy of efficiency. Just a thin layer of limescale inside your boiler or hot water cylinder forces it to work harder and burn more fuel. Softened water keeps the system clean, helping it run efficiently and last longer.

Kinder on Skin & Hair

Many people find softened water leaves their skin less dry and their hair feeling softer. It also means less soap and shampoo to get a good lather.

Cleaner Kitchens & Bathrooms

Say goodbye to scale marks on taps, shower screens, and tiles, and to cloudy glassware. Softened water makes cleaning quicker and keeps surfaces looking better for longer.

Longer-Lasting Appliances

Dishwashers, washing machines, kettles, and coffee machines all suffer in hard water. Softening protects them and reduces the need for descaling.

Professional Installation

We assess your incoming mains, recommend the right size of softener for your household, and install it neatly — usually under the kitchen sink or in a utility space. We fit a separate hard-water tap for drinking water where you'd like one, and make sure everything is plumbed cleanly and tested before we leave.

Kitchen Plumbing

Alongside softeners we handle all kinds of kitchen plumbing — fitting and replacing kitchen taps, filter taps, sinks, dishwashers, washing machines, and waste connections. If you need a wider plumbing fix, see our plumbing repairs service.

Installed, all in: most Doncaster homes land at £1,000–£2,000 supplied and fitted, with the typical three-bed coming in around £1,200–£1,500. A premium unit with a longer warranty and better efficiency takes the total to £1,500–£2,500.

Running costs: block or tablet salt is roughly £10–£15 per 25kg bag, and a typical family uses about one bag a month — so budget £120–£180 a year. Set against that, softened water reduces scale in the boiler and cylinder, extends the life of appliances, and cuts what you spend on descaler, soap and shampoo.

Our Water Softener Installation Process

Step 1: Free Home Survey — We find your incoming mains stop tap, check the available space, identify a suitable drain for the regeneration discharge, and ask about your household size and water usage.

Step 2: Sizing and Recommendation — Softeners are sized by household demand. We recommend a unit that will cope with your peak usage without regenerating constantly, and explain why we have picked it.

Step 3: Written Fixed Quote — An itemised quote covering the unit, all materials, labour, the drinking-water tap if you want one, and commissioning. No obligation.

Step 6: Handover — We show you how to top up the salt, how the bypass works, what the settings do, and hand over the manufacturer warranty paperwork.

Water Softener Servicing, Repairs and Salt

A water softener is a mechanical appliance with a valve head, a resin bed and a timer or meter, and like any appliance it benefits from being looked at. We service and repair softeners across Doncaster — including units we did not install.

Common faults we see: the unit stops regenerating and hard water starts coming through again; a salt bridge forms in the tank so the brine never reaches the resin; the venturi or injector blocks; the timer loses its settings after a power cut; or the resin bed is simply exhausted after ten to fifteen years and needs replacing.

Signs yours needs attention: limescale reappearing on taps and the kettle, soap not lathering the way it used to, salt levels not dropping at all, or the unit running a regeneration cycle far more often than it used to.

Servicing is charged at our standard labour rate and usually takes under an hour. If a part has failed we will tell you what it costs to repair versus replace before we do anything — on an older unit it is not always worth repairing, and we will say so.

Water Softener or Scale Reducer — Which Do You Need?

These are different products and the distinction matters, because scale reducers are often sold as if they were softeners.

A water softener physically removes calcium and magnesium from the water by ion exchange, swapping them for sodium. The water leaving the unit is genuinely soft. You get the full benefit — no new scale anywhere, better lathering, softer skin and laundry, protected boiler and appliances. It needs salt, a drain and a power socket.

A scale reducer or conditioner (electronic, magnetic or phosphate-dosing) does not remove anything. It alters how the minerals behave so they are less likely to stick as hard scale. The water is still chemically hard. These are cheaper, need no drain or salt, and can be worth fitting where a full softener is not practical — but they will not give you soft water, and they will not do much for your skin, your laundry or your glassware.

Our honest view: if you have the space, a drain and a socket, fit a proper softener. If you genuinely cannot, a scale reducer is better than nothing for protecting the boiler, and we will fit one. We would rather tell you that on the survey than sell you the wrong thing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Doncaster a hard-water area?
Yes — Doncaster and most of South Yorkshire have moderately hard to hard water, which is why limescale builds up so quickly on taps, kettles, and inside boilers. A water softener is one of the most effective ways to deal with it.
Where does the softener go?
Most often under the kitchen sink, but it can also go in a utility room, garage, or wherever your mains water enters the property. We'll find the neatest, most practical spot during the assessment.
Can I still drink the water?
Softened water is fine for most people, but many prefer a separate hard-water tap for drinking and cooking. We can fit one as part of the installation if you'd like.
Will it really protect my boiler?
Yes. Limescale is one of the main causes of lost efficiency and breakdowns in hard-water areas. Softened water keeps the heat exchanger and pipework clean, which helps your boiler run efficiently and last longer.
Which areas do you cover?
We cover Doncaster and the surrounding DN postcodes, including Balby, Bessacarr, Cantley, Armthorpe, Bentley, Mexborough, Conisbrough, Thorne, Tickhill, Rossington, and Bawtry.

Running costs are roughly £120–£180 a year in salt. Every job is confirmed with a written fixed quote after a free survey.
How long does a water softener installation take?
Most installations are completed in a single visit. The work involves isolating the mains, fitting the unit with a bypass valve, connecting the regeneration discharge to a drain, running the pipework, then commissioning and testing at the tap. A more awkward location, a long run to a suitable drain, or fitting a separate hard-water drinking tap can extend it.
Do you service and repair existing water softeners?
Yes, including units we did not install. What is the difference between a water softener and a scale reducer?
A softener physically removes calcium and magnesium by ion exchange, so the water leaving it is genuinely soft. A scale reducer or conditioner removes nothing — it alters how the minerals behave so they are less likely to stick. Scale reducers are cheaper and need no salt, drain or power, but they will not give you soft water and will not help your skin, laundry or glassware. How much salt does a water softener use?
A typical family uses about one 25kg bag of block or tablet salt a month, at roughly £10–£15 a bag, so budget £120–£180 a year. Usage depends on household size, water hardness and how efficiently the unit regenerates — which is one of the reasons a better-quality softener costs less to run.

No. Softened water protects a boiler — limescale on the heat exchanger is one of the main causes of lost efficiency and breakdowns in hard-water areas. Manufacturers do not void warranties for softened water.
