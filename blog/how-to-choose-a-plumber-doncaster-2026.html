How to Choose a Plumber in Doncaster (2026): Checks, Quotes, Deposits & Red Flags

✍️ By: Adam Smith, Yorkshire Green Heating

Doncaster Buyer's Guide
Choosing a plumber in Doncaster — the checks that matter before you hand over a deposit
Gas Safe & insurance checks · What a proper quote looks like · Safe staged payments · Red flags

Choosing a plumber in Doncaster is easy when the job is small. Somebody comes, fixes the tap, you pay, done. It gets a lot harder when the job is big — a full bathroom, a mobility shower conversion, a re-pipe, a kitchen move. Suddenly you are handing over a deposit, letting someone rip out a room, and trusting a stranger to come back next Tuesday. That is where people in Doncaster get caught out, and it is almost never because they picked a bad plumber on purpose. It is because nobody told them which questions actually matter.

This guide is written from the other side of the van. We are Yorkshire Green Heating — a Doncaster family firm, Gas Safe registered (638592), rated 9.79/10 across 623 verified Checkatrade reviews and 5.0 across 120 Google reviews. We have been called in to finish other people's abandoned bathrooms often enough to know exactly how it goes wrong. Here is what to check, in the order it matters.

The Quick Version: 7 Checks Before You Book Anyone

  1. Gas work? Verify the Gas Safe number yourself — on the Gas Safe Register site, not on their van.
  2. Ask for the public liability certificate and check it is in date and in the company's name.
  3. Get the quote in writing, itemised, with what is not included spelled out.
  4. Never pay a large cash deposit up front. Materials on account, staged payments against progress.
  5. Ask who is actually doing the work — the person quoting, an employee, or a subcontractor?
  6. Ask what the guarantee covers and for how long, and get it in writing.
  7. Check reviews that are verified, not just counted. Vetted platforms and Google both, ideally.

The rest of this article explains why each one matters and what a good answer sounds like.

1. Registration: What "Registered" Actually Means

"Registered" is doing a lot of unearned work in some adverts. There is no single licence to be a plumber in the UK. Anyone can print cards. What is regulated is gas.

If your job touches a gas appliance — a boiler, a gas hob, a gas fire, a gas cooker connection — the person doing it must be on the Gas Safe Register. Not "Gas Safe trained". Not "working under a Gas Safe engineer". On the register, with an ID card, and with the specific appliance category on the back of that card. It is a criminal offence to work on gas without it, and it is the one check where being polite about it could genuinely cost you your family.

Verifying takes about thirty seconds: type the business name or registration number into the Gas Safe Register's own website. Ours is 638592. If you would like the longer version of this check, including what the two sides of the ID card should say, we wrote a separate guide on how to spot a genuinely Gas Safe registered engineer in Doncaster.

For water-only work there is no legal register, but there are voluntary schemes (WaterSafe, and the approved-contractor schemes run through the water regulations) plus vetted trade platforms. Those are a decent proxy for "someone has checked this firm exists, is insured, and does not have a trail of complaints".

2. Insurance: The Boring Check That Saves You Thousands

Ask for a copy of the public liability certificate. Any legitimate firm will send it without blinking — ours is £5,000,000 of cover and we email it out on request.

Three things to look at when it arrives:

  • The name on it matches the company you are hiring. A certificate in a different trading name is a flag.
  • The expiry date is in the future, and ideally beyond the end of your job.
  • The level of cover. £1m is common; £2m–£5m is what you want if someone is opening up pipework in your home.

Why it matters: a slipped joint under a first-floor bathroom floor can write off a ceiling, a kitchen and a floor covering in an afternoon. If the plumber is not insured, your options are their goodwill or the small claims court. Neither will dry your plasterboard.

3. The Quote: What a Proper One Looks Like

A verbal "about three grand, mate" is not a quote. It is a conversation. On a bathroom or a heating job you want it written down, and you want it to answer three questions.

What is included

Labour, materials, waste removal, making good. Does "bathroom fitted" include tiling? Plastering? Electrics for the shower or extractor? Taking the old suite to the tip? On a Doncaster bathroom, those four items are the usual gap between the price quoted and the price paid.

What is excluded

A good quote says so out loud. "Price excludes any repairs to joists found once the floor is up" is honest and normal. "Price excludes anything unforeseen" is not a quote, it is a blank cheque.

Fixed price or estimate

For installation work — bathrooms, boilers, mobility conversions — insist on a fixed price after a survey. For repairs and diagnostics, an hourly rate with a call-out is perfectly reasonable, because nobody can price a leak they have not seen yet. If you want the going rates for repair work in Doncaster, we publish them in our 2026 plumbing repair cost guide, and the fitted-price bands for bathrooms are in our bathroom renovation cost guide.

A useful test: ask what happens to the price if the job takes two days longer than expected. On a genuine fixed price the answer is "nothing changes". If the answer wanders, you have an estimate, not a quote — price it accordingly.

4. Deposits and Staged Payments: The Single Biggest Trap

This is where the horror stories come from, and it is worth being blunt about it.

On a job of any size, some money up front is completely normal. A bathroom suite has to be bought before it can be fitted, and no small firm can float thousands of pounds of other people's tiles. What is not normal is a large cash deposit with nothing to show for it.

A sensible structure on a Doncaster bathroom or mobility conversion looks like this:

StageWhat is reasonableWhat to be wary of
Before work startsA materials payment, itemised, roughly matching the cost of goods being ordered"50% cash up front" with no breakdown
Mid-jobA staged payment once first fix is complete and visibleRequests for more money before the previous stage is finished
On completionThe balance, after you have seen it working and signed it offBeing asked to pay in full before snagging
MethodBank transfer or card, with a receipt or invoice each timeCash only, or a personal account in a different name

The pattern behind almost every abandoned-bathroom story is the same: a big deposit, then work stops, then the payments requested get further ahead of the work delivered. Keeping payment behind progress is the one rule that protects you, and no honest firm will object to it.

5. Who Is Actually Turning Up?

Ask directly: is the person who quoted the job the person doing it? On bigger work the honest answer is often "me and my lad", or "I fit it, and my electrician does the spur for the shower". That is fine — it is how good small firms work. What you want to avoid is discovering on day one that the job has been passed to a subcontractor you have never met and whose insurance and registration you have not checked.

Related question, and a genuinely useful one: how many jobs will they be running at once? A firm juggling five bathrooms is why your fitter disappears for three days. Ask how long the job will take, and how many days a week they will be on site. Write the answer down.

6. Guarantees: Ask What, Not Just How Long

"Guaranteed" on its own means nothing. There are two separate things being guaranteed on most plumbing work, and they are not the same:

  • The manufacturer's warranty on the product — the boiler, the shower, the pump. Often 2–10 years, but conditional on registered installation by a qualified engineer and, in most cases, on annual servicing. That last condition catches a lot of people out.
  • The installer's workmanship guarantee — the firm's own promise to come back and fix their own errors. Usually 12 months on plumbing work; longer on installations.

Ask for both in writing. Then ask the harder question: what does the workmanship guarantee actually cover, and how do I claim on it? A guarantee from a firm that stops answering the phone is decoration. This is a strong argument for using a firm with a long, public, verified review history — a business with hundreds of reviews attached to its name has a great deal to lose by ignoring you.

7. Reviews: Verified Beats Numerous

Reviews are good evidence when you read them properly.

  • Verified platforms (Checkatrade, Which? Trusted Traders, and similar) check that the reviewer was a real customer and vet the firm's insurance and identity before listing them. Harder to fake.
  • Google reviews are unverified but public, hard to remove, and show how a firm responds when something goes wrong. Read the replies to the middling reviews — that tells you more than any five-star.
  • Recency and consistency matter more than the raw count. Forty reviews spread evenly over two years is stronger evidence than two hundred that all landed in one month three years ago.

For reference, ours are public in both places: 9.79/10 from 623 verified Checkatrade reviews, and 5.0 from 120 Google reviews.

Red Flags: The Short List

  • Cash only, or payment to a personal account in a different name.
  • A large deposit demanded before any materials are ordered or shown.
  • No written quote, or a quote with no address, company number or VAT position on it.
  • Pressure to decide today, or a "discount if you book now".
  • Refusal or reluctance to produce an insurance certificate or Gas Safe card.
  • A price dramatically below everyone else's for the same specification. Somebody has left something out, and it will be found later.
  • No landline, no fixed address, no company registration you can look up.

None of these are proof of anything on their own. Two or three together, on a job worth thousands, is a reason to get another quote.

Local Firm or National Chain?

Both have a place. National chains are genuinely useful at 2am on a bank holiday when nobody local is picking up, and their call centres are always staffed. What you pay for that is a call-out and hourly rate set nationally, and an engineer who is unlikely to be the same one who came last time.

A local Doncaster firm gives you continuity — the same engineer, who remembers your system, who can drop back in when the new shower valve needs a tweak, and who lives close enough that their reputation in DN postcodes actually matters to them. For anything planned, that continuity is worth more than a 24-hour phone line. For a genuine 3am emergency, take whoever answers — and see our guide to when you actually need an emergency plumber before you make the call.

Questions Worth Asking, Word for Word

Copy these into a note on your phone and work down them on the survey visit:

  • "Can you send me your public liability certificate?"
  • "Is this a fixed price after survey, or an estimate?"
  • "What is specifically not included in this price?"
  • "What is the payment schedule, and what is the deposit for?"
  • "Who will be on site, and how many days a week?"
  • "What is your workmanship guarantee, and how long?"
  • "If something needs fixing in six months, what happens?"

Any good firm in Doncaster will have quick, unhesitating answers to all seven. The hesitation is the information.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check a plumber in Doncaster is Gas Safe registered?
Search the business name or registration number on the Gas Safe Register's own website rather than relying on a logo on the van or a card handed to you. Yorkshire Green Heating is registered under 638592. Ask to see the engineer's ID card as well, and check the back of it, which lists the specific gas appliance categories they are qualified to work on. Working on gas without being on the register is a criminal offence.
How much deposit should I pay a plumber before work starts?
A materials payment that roughly matches the cost of the goods being ordered is normal and reasonable, and it should be itemised. What is not normal is a large percentage of the total demanded in cash before anything is ordered or delivered. Keep payments behind progress: materials on account, a staged payment once first fix is visibly complete, and the balance only after you have seen the work finished and working. Pay by bank transfer or card so there is a record, and get an invoice each time.
What should a written plumbing quote include?
A proper quote is itemised and says what is included, what is excluded, and whether it is a fixed price or an estimate. On a bathroom, the items that most often cause disputes are tiling, plastering, electrical work for showers and extractors, and removal of the old suite and waste, so check each of those explicitly. It should also carry the company name, address and company number. For installation work insist on a fixed price after a survey; for repairs, an hourly rate plus call-out is reasonable because the work cannot be seen in advance.
What insurance should a plumber in Doncaster have?
Public liability insurance is the key one. Ask for a copy of the certificate and check that the company name matches the firm you are hiring, that the expiry date is beyond the end of your job, and that the level of cover is adequate. One million pounds is common; two to five million is preferable for work that involves opening up pipework inside your home. Yorkshire Green Heating carries five million pounds of public liability cover and will email the certificate on request.
What is the difference between a manufacturer's warranty and a workmanship guarantee?
A manufacturer's warranty covers the product itself, such as a boiler, shower or pump, and is usually conditional on registered installation by a qualified engineer and on annual servicing. A workmanship guarantee is the installer's own promise to return and correct their own errors, typically twelve months on plumbing work and longer on installations. You want both in writing, and you should ask specifically what the workmanship guarantee covers and how to claim on it.
What are the biggest red flags when hiring a plumber?
Cash-only payment or payment into a personal account in a different name, a large deposit demanded before materials are ordered, no written quote or a quote with no company details, pressure to decide immediately, reluctance to produce an insurance certificate or Gas Safe card, and a price dramatically below every other quote for the same specification. Any one of these might have an innocent explanation, but two or three together on a job worth thousands is a reason to get another quote.
Should I use a local Doncaster plumber or a national chain?
National chains are useful for genuine out-of-hours emergencies because their phone lines are always staffed, but you pay nationally set call-out and hourly rates and are unlikely to see the same engineer twice. A local Doncaster firm gives you continuity: the same engineer, who knows your system, can return quickly for adjustments, and whose reputation in the DN postcodes matters to them commercially. For planned work such as bathrooms, boilers or mobility conversions, that continuity is generally worth more than a 24-hour call centre.
