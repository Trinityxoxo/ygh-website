Will a Smart Thermostat Work With My Boiler? A Plain-English Guide (2026)

📅 Published: October 30, 2026
✍️ By: Adam Smith, Yorkshire Green Heating
🔖 Category: Smart Heating
← Back to Blog
Smart Heating Guide
Will it work with my boiler? Almost always. Here’s how to check.
Combi · System · Regular · Older boilers · Hive, Nest & tado° X · Gas Safe 638592

“Will it work with my boiler?” is the question we get asked most about smart thermostats. The short answer is yes: Hive, Nest and tado° X will work with almost any working gas boiler fitted in the last 15 to 20 years, and with plenty of older ones too. What changes is which version you need, how much it can save you, and whether the boiler itself is the thing holding your heating back.

This guide walks you through it in plain English, the same way we do at a survey in Doncaster. If you’re still choosing between the three brands, start with our Hive vs Nest vs tado° comparison and come back here.

Step 1: Find Your Boiler’s Make and Model

Everything starts here. The make and model are printed on the front of the boiler casing, or on a sticker underneath or behind the front flap. You’ll usually see something like “Worcester Greenstar 30i”, “Ideal Logic Combi 30” or “Baxi 800”. Take a photo of it on your phone. With that one photo, a heating engineer can tell you almost everything below in a couple of minutes.

Step 2: Work Out What Type of Boiler You Have

There are three common types in Doncaster homes, and the type decides which version of the thermostat you need.

Combi boiler (no hot water tank)

If you get hot water straight from the boiler and there’s no hot water cylinder in an airing cupboard, you have a combi. This is the easiest case. The smart thermostat only needs to control your heating, because hot water comes on instantly whenever you turn a tap. All three brands work, and it’s usually the quickest fit.

System or regular boiler (with a hot water cylinder)

If there’s a hot water tank in a cupboard, you have a system boiler or a regular (“heat-only”) boiler. Here the smart thermostat needs to control two things: the heating and when the tank gets heated. Each brand sells a version for this, and it matters which one you buy. Fit the heating-only version on a cylinder system and you lose your hot water timer. We look at the valves and cylinder thermostat in the cupboard before quoting, so you get the right kit first time.

Older boilers, back boilers and unusual setups

Most older gas boilers still work fine with a smart thermostat, because the thermostat just tells the boiler “on” or “off”, exactly like the old dial on the wall. Back boilers behind a gas fire, very old wiring centres and some oil or LPG setups can need a bit more thought. It’s rarely a “no”, but it’s worth a look before you spend money.

Step 3: Can Your Boiler Be Turned Down, Not Just On and Off?

This is where the bigger savings are, and where most people get caught out.

A basic thermostat treats the boiler like a light switch: full power, then off, then full power again. Many modern boilers can do something smarter. They can be told to run gently at a lower flame for longer, which uses less gas and is kinder to the boiler. The most common way a thermostat does this is called OpenTherm. You don’t need to remember the name. You just need to know whether your boiler and your thermostat both support it.

  • tado° X and Nest can turn a compatible boiler down, not just on and off.
  • Hive works on an on/off basis. It’s still a good, simple thermostat, it just can’t do this part.
  • Your boiler has to support it too. Many Ideal, Baxi and Worcester models do; some brands use their own system instead. This is exactly what the make-and-model photo tells us.

If your boiler can be turned down, that’s a strong reason to pick tado° X or Nest. If it can’t, Hive gives you most of the everyday benefits for a little less money. Our tado° X guide explains the difference this makes day to day.

Step 4: Check the Boiler Isn’t the Real Problem

A smart thermostat controls when and where your heating runs. It can’t fix a boiler or a heating system that isn’t working properly. Before spending money on controls, look out for these:

  • Radiators cold at the bottom or patchy: usually sludge in the system. A powerflush or system clean will do more for your comfort than any thermostat. See our central heating page.
  • Boiler losing pressure, making noises or cutting out: get it looked at first. We cover boiler repairs across Doncaster.
  • No service in the last year: a boiler service before winter catches small faults early, and many manufacturer warranties need one every year.
  • Boiler over 12 to 15 years old: it’s probably costing you more in gas than a new one would. Read repair or replace? before you decide.

Replacing Your Boiler Soon? Do Both Together

If a new boiler is on the cards in the next year or so, it’s worth doing the thermostat at the same time. In England, when a new combi boiler is fitted, the installation has to include an extra energy-saving measure under the Boiler Plus rules, and a smart thermostat with automation and optimisation is one of the ways to meet that. Choosing the boiler and the controls together also means you can pick a boiler that can be turned down, and get the full saving from day one. Our new boiler installation page has fixed prices, and the new boiler cost guide breaks them down.

Can I Fit It Myself?

Some people do. The thermostat on the wall is the easy bit. The part that connects to your boiler is wired into the boiler’s controls on mains voltage, and on cylinder systems it often has to be wired into the valves too. If you’re not confident with mains wiring and heating controls, it’s a job for a heating engineer. A badly wired receiver is one of the most common reasons we get called out to “smart heating that isn’t working”.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a smart thermostat work with a combi boiler?
Yes. Combi boilers are the easiest type to fit a smart thermostat to, because the thermostat only needs to control the heating. Hot water comes straight from the boiler whenever you turn a tap. Hive, Nest and tado X all work with combi boilers.
Will a smart thermostat work with a boiler and a hot water tank?
Yes, but you need the version that controls hot water as well as heating. System and regular boilers with a hot water cylinder need the thermostat to time when the tank is heated. Fitting the heating-only version loses your hot water timer, so check before you buy.
Do I need a new boiler to get a smart thermostat?
No. Almost any working gas boiler from the last 15 to 20 years will work with Hive, Nest or tado X, and many older ones do too. A newer boiler that can be turned down rather than just switched on and off will give you bigger savings, but it isn't required.
What is OpenTherm?
OpenTherm is a way for a thermostat to tell your boiler to run gently at a lower flame instead of just switching it on and off. It saves gas and is kinder to the boiler. Both the boiler and the thermostat must support it. tado X and Nest can use it on compatible boilers; Hive works on an on/off basis.
Can I fit a smart thermostat myself?
Some people do, but the part that connects to the boiler is wired into the boiler's controls on mains voltage, and cylinder systems often need wiring into the valves too. If you are not confident with mains wiring and heating controls, use a heating engineer.
