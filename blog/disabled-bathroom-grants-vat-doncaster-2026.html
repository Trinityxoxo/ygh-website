The Two Kinds of Help Most People Miss

They're completely separate, and you may qualify for one, both, or neither:

  1. A Disabled Facilities Grant (DFG) — public money from your council towards the cost of the adaptation, worth up to £30,000.
  2. 0% VAT relief — if you're disabled or chronically sick, the qualifying work is zero-rated, so you don't pay the 20% VAT on it at all.

Getting these two confused is the single most common thing we see. Let's take them one at a time.

The Disabled Facilities Grant (Up to £30,000)

The Disabled Facilities Grant is a statutory, government-backed grant administered by your local council — for most readers of this, the City of Doncaster Council. It helps pay for changes to your home so a disabled person can live there more safely and independently, and a bathroom conversion — ripping out a bath and fitting a level-access shower, for example — is one of the most common things it's used for.

Here's what actually matters about it:

Question The 2026 answer
How much? Up to £30,000 in England. Councils have discretionary powers to top up beyond that where the work costs more — policies vary, so ask.
Who runs it? Your local council. You apply to them, and an occupational therapist usually assesses what's needed.
Is it means-tested? For an adult, yes — on the income and savings of the disabled person and their partner. For a child under 18, it is not means-tested at all.
Do I pay it back? Generally no, though larger grants can carry a charge on the property in some circumstances — the council will tell you.
Can I start the work first? No — and this is the big one. Apply and get approval before any work begins. Work you've already paid for usually can't be funded retrospectively.

The mistake that costs people thousands: booking the work in, getting it done, and then asking the council about a grant. It almost never works in that order. If you think a Disabled Facilities Grant might apply, contact the council's home improvement or adaptations team before you commit to the work. We're very happy to provide the fixed-price quote the council will ask for as part of your application — that part we can do straight away.

You can read the official rules and find your council's application route on the government's own page: gov.uk/disabled-facilities-grants. If the grant route looks likely for you, that's the first call to make.

0% VAT Relief for Disabled or Chronically Sick People

This one is separate from any grant, it's often missed entirely, and it can knock a fifth off the bill on its own.

Under long-standing HMRC rules (VAT Notice 701/7), the work of providing, extending or adapting a bathroom, washroom or lavatory to suit the condition of a disabled or chronically sick person, in their own private home, can be zero-rated for VAT. In plain terms: you don't pay the 20% VAT on the qualifying work.

The part people get wrong is who qualifies:

  • It's not about age. Being elderly on its own does not qualify. It's about being disabled or chronically sick.
  • You don't have to be a wheelchair user. The test is a physical or mental impairment with a long-term and substantial effect on everyday activities — or a condition the medical profession treats as a chronic sickness (diabetes is a commonly cited example).
  • Temporary doesn't count. A broken leg or a short-term injury isn't enough — it has to be long-term.

You don't need HMRC's permission and you don't need to prove anything to us in advance. In practice, the customer signs a short, simple written declaration confirming they're eligible, and we apply the zero rate to the qualifying part of the quote. We handle that paperwork for you — it's a single form, not a bureaucratic ordeal.

What VAT relief covers — and what it doesn't. The relief applies to the work that's necessary because of the person's condition (the level-access shower, the adaptation, associated building work to make it usable). Purely cosmetic upgrades chosen for taste rather than need aren't covered. When we quote a mobility bathroom we set it out clearly, so you can see exactly which part is zero-rated.

Can You Use Both Together?

Yes — they're not mutually exclusive. The VAT relief reduces what the work costs; the Disabled Facilities Grant helps pay for what's left. Where a grant is funding qualifying adaptation work for an eligible person, that work is generally zero-rated too, which stretches the grant further. The two stack rather than cancel out, and a well-planned mobility bathroom in Doncaster often uses both.

The order matters, though: sort the grant application first (because you can't start work before approval), and the VAT relief is applied at the point we quote and invoice.

What an Accessible Bathroom in Doncaster Actually Looks Like

"Mobility bathroom" doesn't mean clinical or institutional. Done well, it just looks like a modern, tidy bathroom that happens to be far safer to use. Depending on what you need, the options include:

  • A level-access (walk-in) shower or wet room — no step or tray lip to climb over, so you can walk or wheel straight in. Our walk-in shower vs wet room guide explains which suits which home.
  • A bath-to-shower conversion — the most common job we do for older customers: the old bath comes out, a safe walk-in shower goes in, usually in a day or two. More in our bath-to-shower guide for older homeowners.
  • Grab rails and a fold-down or fixed shower seat — positioned where you actually need them, not just where they're easy to fit.
  • Slip-resistant flooring, a raised, comfort-height WC, lever or thermostatic taps that are easy on stiff hands and guard against scalding, and good lighting.
  • A low-threshold or half-height door and sensible turning space where a walker or wheelchair is used.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Disabled Facilities Grant for a bathroom in Doncaster?
The Disabled Facilities Grant is worth up to £30,000 in England, and a bathroom adaptation such as a bath-to-shower conversion is one of the most common things it's used for. It's administered by your local council, and for adults it's means-tested on the income and savings of the disabled person and their partner. For a child under 18 it is not means-tested. Councils can sometimes top up beyond £30,000 at their discretion. Always apply and get approval before any work starts.
Do I qualify for 0% VAT on a disabled or accessible bathroom?
You can have qualifying bathroom adaptation work zero-rated for VAT if you are disabled or chronically sick — meaning a physical or mental impairment with a long-term, substantial effect on everyday activities, or a condition the medical profession treats as a chronic sickness. Being elderly on its own does not qualify, and temporary conditions don't count. You sign a short written eligibility declaration; you don't need HMRC's permission. We apply the relief to the qualifying part of your quote.
Can I claim both a grant and VAT relief?
Yes. They're separate. The 0% VAT relief reduces the cost of the qualifying work, and a Disabled Facilities Grant helps pay for what remains — so they stack rather than cancel out. Sort the grant application first, because work generally can't be funded retrospectively, and the VAT relief is applied when we quote and invoice.
Do I have to be a wheelchair user to get VAT relief?
No. The test is whether you have a physical or mental impairment with a long-term and substantial adverse effect on your ability to carry out everyday activities, or a chronic condition recognised by the medical profession. Many people who aren't wheelchair users still qualify. It's a self-declaration — you decide whether you meet the definition, and can check the GOV.UK guidance (VAT Notice 701/7) if you're unsure.
How do I apply for a Disabled Facilities Grant?
Contact your local council's home improvement or adaptations team — for most Doncaster residents that's the City of Doncaster Council — before booking any work. There's usually an occupational therapist assessment of what's needed, and the council will ask for a quote for the proposed work. We're happy to provide that fixed-price quote in a format the council accepts. The official starting point is gov.uk/disabled-facilities-grants.
What does an accessible bathroom usually include?
Common features are a level-access walk-in shower or wet room with no step to climb over, grab rails and a shower seat positioned where you need them, slip-resistant flooring, a comfort-height WC, easy-to-use lever or thermostatic (anti-scald) taps, and good lighting. It's tailored to the person — we design around what actually helps, not a one-size template, and it ends up looking like a smart modern bathroom rather than a clinical one.
