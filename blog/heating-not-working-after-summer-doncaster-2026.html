Heating Not Working After Summer? A Doncaster Switch-On Guide (2026)

Turned the heating on and nothing happened? Start here.
Seven safe checks · When to call an engineer · Fixed-price repairs · Gas Safe 638592

Every October the same thing happens across Doncaster. The first cold snap arrives, thousands of people turn the heating back on after five months off, and a fair few of them discover it does not work. The boiler will not fire, the radiators stay cold, or everything comes on but the house never gets warm.

Most switch-on problems are minor and some you can sort yourself in five minutes. This guide walks through the common ones in the order we would check them, what you can safely try, and the point at which you need a Gas Safe registered engineer.

One rule before anything else: if you can smell gas, do not touch any switches. Open the windows, turn the gas off at the meter if you can, leave the property and call the National Gas Emergency line on 0800 111 999. Never take the boiler casing off yourself — working on a gas appliance is only legal for a Gas Safe registered engineer.

Why Heating Fails After the Summer

A boiler that has done nothing but heat water since April has had its heating side sitting idle for months. Three things tend to go wrong in that time.

Pumps and diverter valves stick. The circulating pump and the diverter valve (the part inside a combi that switches between hot water and heating) can seize after a long period of not moving, particularly on hard-water systems like most of Doncaster's.

Pressure drops. Sealed systems lose a little pressure over time. Over a summer it can drift low enough that the boiler locks out the moment it is asked to heat.

Air and sludge settle. Air collects at the top of radiators and sludge settles at the bottom. Neither shows up until the heating is asked to run.

Seven Checks to Try First

1. Is the boiler getting power and gas? Check the display is lit, the fused spur next to the boiler is on, and other gas appliances such as the hob are working. If you are on a prepayment meter, check there is credit.

2. Check the pressure gauge. On most combi boilers the gauge should read between 1 and 1.5 bar when the system is cold. Below about 0.8 bar many boilers will refuse to fire. Your boiler manual will show how to top up using the filling loop. If the pressure keeps dropping after you top it up, there is a leak somewhere and it needs looking at.

3. Check the thermostat and timer. It sounds obvious, but this is the single most common "fault" we get called out to in October. Clocks change on the last Sunday of the month, programmers reset after power cuts, and smart thermostats can drop off the Wi-Fi or run flat batteries over the summer. Turn the room thermostat a few degrees above the room temperature and set the heating to constant on.

4. Reset the boiler once. If the display shows a fault code, look it up in the manual and press reset once. If it locks out again, stop — repeated resetting can mask a genuine fault, and the code is useful information for the engineer.

5. Check the condensate pipe. This is more of a winter problem than an October one, but if the boiler shows a blockage or ignition fault on a frosty morning, the plastic pipe running outside may have frozen. Pouring warm (not boiling) water along the outside section usually clears it.

6. Bleed the radiators. If radiators are cold at the top and warm at the bottom, they have air in them. With the heating off, open the bleed valve with a radiator key until water appears, then close it. Recheck the boiler pressure afterwards — bleeding lets pressure out.

7. Check the radiator valves. A thermostatic radiator valve that has sat on one setting all summer can stick shut. Turn it fully open and back a few times. If the pin underneath the head is stuck, a gentle tap often frees it.

Signs You Need an Engineer

Stop and call a Gas Safe engineer if any of these apply:

The boiler keeps locking out on the same fault code after one reset.

You can hear the boiler firing but the radiators stay cold. That usually points to a seized pump or a stuck diverter valve.

Hot water works but the heating does not, or the other way round. On a combi this is very often the diverter valve.

The pressure keeps dropping after you top it up, which means a leak in the system or a failing pressure relief valve.

Banging, kettling or whistling from the boiler once the heating runs, which usually means sludge or scale on the heat exchanger.

Radiators are cold at the bottom across several rooms. That is sludge, not air, and bleeding will not fix it. Our powerflush guide explains the options and what they cost.

Water leaking from the boiler, from any pipe under it, or from the overflow pipe outside.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my heating not working when I turn it on after the summer?
After months idle, the most common causes are low boiler pressure, a thermostat or timer that has reset or lost its Wi-Fi connection, a pump or diverter valve that has stuck, or air in the radiators. Check the pressure gauge, the thermostat and the timer first. If the boiler fires but the radiators stay cold, or it keeps locking out, call a Gas Safe engineer.
What should my boiler pressure be?
On most combi boilers the gauge should read between 1 and 1.5 bar when the system is cold. Below about 0.8 bar many boilers will not fire. You can top it up using the filling loop as shown in your boiler manual. If it keeps dropping after you top it up, there is a leak or a failing component and it needs an engineer.
Why do I have hot water but no heating?
On a combi boiler this is very often the diverter valve, the part that switches the boiler between hot water and heating. It can stick after a summer of not being used. Check the thermostat and timer first; if they are fine, it needs a Gas Safe engineer. A diverter valve replacement is typically £180 to £280 fitted in Doncaster.
Should I bleed my radiators before turning the heating on?
Only if they need it. Bleed a radiator when it is cold at the top and warm at the bottom, which means trapped air. Do it with the heating off, then recheck the boiler pressure because bleeding lets pressure out. If radiators are cold at the bottom instead, that is sludge and bleeding will not help.
How much does a boiler repair cost in Doncaster?
Every repair is quoted as a fixed price after diagnosis, before any work starts. Our diagnostic visit is £80 to £120 inc VAT and is credited against the repair if you go ahead. As a guide, fitted and inc VAT: a pressure sensor or relief valve is £120 to £180, an expansion vessel £150 to £240, a diverter valve £180 to £280 and a pump £220 to £360.
Is it safe to reset my boiler?
Pressing the reset button once, as described in the boiler manual, is safe. If the boiler locks out again on the same fault, stop and call a Gas Safe engineer rather than resetting repeatedly. Never remove the boiler casing yourself. If you smell gas, leave the property and call the National Gas Emergency line on 0800 111 999.
When should I get my boiler serviced?
Once a year, ideally in late summer or early autumn before the heating season starts. A service checks the pressure, expansion vessel, condensate, pump and diverter valve, which are the parts most likely to fail at switch-on. Our annual boiler service is £114 inc VAT.
